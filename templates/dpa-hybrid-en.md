# DPA Template — Tier 3 (Hybrid): SCCs (2021/915) Intact + Commercial Framework Layer — English

## What this template is

A wrapper that **incorporates the full, unmodified Standard Contractual Clauses adopted by Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/915** — Sections I, II **and III** (Clause 10 included) — as the data-protection substance of the agreement, and adds an extensive **commercial framework layer (Section 4)** that sits outside the Clauses for matters they do not address (term, commercial termination rights, transition assistance, liability allocation, indemnification, insurance). Section 4 is permitted under Clause 2(b) of the Clauses because it is additional to, and does not contradict, the Clauses. It never replaces, narrows, or modifies any provision of Sections I, II or III — that would be barred by Clause 2(a), which permits only completing or updating the Annexes.

**Presumption of compliance.** The Art. 28(7) GDPR compliance presumption attaches to the Clauses as published in the OJ, in their entirety, because they are adopted unmodified. Because this template keeps Sections I, II and III intact, the presumption is retained in full — the same as Tier 2 (Strict). Any deviation from this architecture (editing a Clause, or drafting Section 4 so that it narrows, overrides, or duplicates-with-a-conflict a Clause 10 or Section II obligation) loses the presumption and turns the document into a bespoke Art. 28(3) contract to be assessed on its own merits — at that point, use Tier 1 (Commercial) instead and draft the Art. 28(3) substance directly.

## When to use this template

Use Tier 3 (Hybrid) for **the most engagements where 2021/915 is on the table and the commercial mechanics need to go beyond a minimal overlay**. It is the practical sweet spot:

- Negotiated B2B contracts where the controller wants the full safe-harbor benefit of the Clauses (same as Tier 2) but the parties need a richer commercial layer than Tier 2's minimal Section 4 — term, commercial termination rights, transition mechanics, sophisticated liability allocation (super-cap, indemnities, insurance covenants).
- Strategic vendor relationships with multi-year terms, transition obligations, exit assistance — none of which the Clauses address, and which the parties want spelled out rather than left to the Main Agreement.
- Counterparty has accepted 2021/915 in principle but wants extensive commercial mechanics negotiated alongside it, without touching the Clauses themselves.

For a minimal commercial overlay with the same full presumption, use **Tier 2 — Strict** (`dpa-strict-en.md`) — same safe-harbor posture, thinner Section 4. For purely commercial work without the 2021/915 anchor, use **Tier 1** (`dpa-commercial-en.md`).

## How to use this template

1. Load `references/2021-915-commission-text-en.md` for the clause-by-clause practitioner guide.
2. Confirm the official OJ-EN text (binding) at https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32021D0915.
3. Walk Section 1.5 (Choices) and lock in selections.
4. Populate Annexes I–IV from intake (Schedules 1–4 below).
5. **Populate Section 4 (commercial framework layer)** — the terms in this template are a starting point at T2 (balanced); adjust per perspective using `references/negotiation-fallbacks.md`. Walk every Section 4 clause against Clause 10 and Sections I–II as a final non-contradiction check (Section 4.1 below is the guard clause; use it as the checklist).
6. Sign and execute.

The Commission text for Sections I, II and III is incorporated by reference (Section 1.1 below) and is **not reproduced**.

## Critical drafting principle

**Sections I, II and III of the SCCs (the Clauses) MUST NOT be modified.** All commercial flexibility lives in:
- The Section 1.5 choices (which the Clauses themselves contemplate).
- The completed Annexes (which the Clauses themselves require).
- Section 4 of this DPA (the commercial framework layer, permitted under Clause 2(b) — additive only, never contradicting).

If you find yourself wanting to modify Clause 10 or Section II language (e.g. weaken the instructions clause, narrow the audit right, cap a Section II obligation, make Section 4 the *exclusive* termination mechanism in place of Clause 10), Tier 3 is no longer the right tool — switch to Tier 1 (Commercial).

## SECTION 4 — COMMERCIAL FRAMEWORK TERMS (OUTSIDE THE CLAUSES) 💼

> 💼 *This Section 4 sits outside Sections I, II and III of the Clauses. It is permitted under Clause 2(b) of the Clauses because it is additional to, and does not contradict, the Clauses. It does not modify, amend, replace, narrow, or serve as a substitute for any provision of Sections I, II or III — including Clause 10. Where anything in this Section 4 is read as inconsistent with the Clauses, Clause 4 of the Clauses resolves the conflict in favour of the Clauses.*

### 4.1 Non-contradiction guard

(a) The commercial termination rights in Section 4.3 are **in addition to**, and do not limit, restrict, or serve as the exclusive termination mechanism in place of, the Controller's rights under Clause 10 of the Clauses (non-compliance and termination). Clause 10 remains available to the Controller at all times, regardless of whether the conditions for termination under Section 4.3 are met.

(b) The liability caps and limitations in Section 4.7 do not limit (i) the Processor's obligations under the Clauses, or (ii) the Parties' liability to data subjects under Article 82 GDPR, in each case regardless of the cap or limitation stated in Section 4.7.

(c) Nothing in this Section 4 shall be read as modifying, amending, superseding, or narrowing any provision of Sections I, II or III of the Clauses (or the Annexes). In case of any conflict, Sections I, II and III of the Clauses (and the Annexes) prevail per Clause 4 of the Clauses.

### 4.2 Term

This DPA enters into force on the Effective Date and remains in force for the duration of the Services under the Main Agreement, plus any post-termination period required to give effect to Section 4.5 below. This Section 4.2 does not affect the operation of Clause 10, which applies independently of the DPA's term.

### 4.3 Commercial termination rights (in addition to Clause 10)

(a) The Controller may terminate this DPA on thirty (30) days' written notice in case of material breach by the Processor of this DPA (including the Clauses) that has not been cured within the notice period. Where the breach affects the data-protection substance and is not capable of cure, the Controller may terminate immediately on written notice. This right is additional to, and does not limit, the Controller's rights under Clause 10.

(b) The Processor may terminate this DPA on thirty (30) days' written notice in case of material breach by the Controller of its obligations under this DPA that has not been cured within the notice period.

(c) Termination of this DPA on the basis of (a) or (b) above entitles the terminating Party to terminate the corresponding portion of the Main Agreement on the same basis without penalty.

### 4.4 Termination for change in law or supervisory authority intervention

Either Party may terminate this DPA on thirty (30) days' written notice (or such shorter period as required by law) where:

(a) a change in applicable data-protection law renders performance of this DPA materially impossible or unlawful, and the Parties cannot in good faith agree on a contractual amendment within sixty (60) days; or

(b) a competent supervisory authority issues a binding order, decision, or determination that prohibits or materially restricts the processing under this DPA.

### 4.5 Operational detail for return or deletion of personal data

This Section 4.5 supplements Clause 10 of the Clauses — which governs the substantive obligation to return or delete personal data at the Controller's choice — with operational detail (timeline, certification, backup handling) that the Clauses do not address. It does not modify Clause 10; in case of any inconsistency between this Section 4.5 and Clause 10, Clause 10 prevails per Clause 4 of the Clauses.

(a) Upon termination of this DPA, the Processor shall, at the choice of the Controller exercised in writing within thirty (30) days of termination:

(i) return all personal data to the Controller in a commonly used, machine-readable format; or
(ii) delete all personal data in its possession or control,

in each case including all copies in production, archive, and backup systems, and shall provide written certification of compliance to the Controller.

(b) Personal data in backup systems shall be deleted upon expiry of the standard backup rotation period (which shall not exceed ninety (90) days from termination); restored backups shall be re-deleted promptly. Until expiry, backups shall be access-restricted and not used for any other purpose.

(c) The Processor may retain personal data only to the extent and for such period as required by Union or Member State law to which the Processor is subject. The Processor shall identify the law and the retention period to the Controller in advance, ensure such personal data is access-restricted and used only for the legally required purpose, and delete the personal data on expiry of the retention period.

### 4.6 Transition assistance

For a period of up to `{{60–180}}` days following termination, the Processor shall provide reasonable transition assistance at the Controller's request, including data export, knowledge transfer, and parallel-running of services where practicable. Transition assistance is `{{included in fees / chargeable at agreed rates as set out in the Main Agreement}}`.

### 4.7 Liability allocation

`[ALT 1: controller-favorable]` Notwithstanding any contrary provision in the Main Agreement, the Processor's aggregate liability for damages arising out of or in connection with breaches of this DPA (including the Clauses), breaches of applicable data-protection law, or personal data breaches shall be capped at an amount equal to two (2) times the fees paid or payable by the Controller under the Main Agreement in the twelve (12) months preceding the event giving rise to liability. This cap is in addition to (not in lieu of) the general liability cap under the Main Agreement and shall not apply to liability for: (a) gross negligence or wilful misconduct; (b) breach of confidentiality; (c) infringement of third-party intellectual property rights; or (d) any liability that cannot be limited or excluded under applicable law. For the avoidance of doubt, and per Section 4.1(b) above, this cap does not limit the Processor's obligations under the Clauses or the Parties' liability to data subjects under Article 82 GDPR.

`[ALT 2: processor-favorable]` The Processor's aggregate liability for damages arising out of or in connection with breaches of this DPA shall be subject to the general liability cap under the Main Agreement. For the avoidance of doubt, and per Section 4.1(b) above, this cap does not limit the Processor's obligations under the Clauses or the Parties' liability to data subjects under Article 82 GDPR.

# Practitioner's note (for OneZero Legal client deliverables)

Tier 3 (Hybrid) is the practical sweet spot for engagements where 2021/915 is on the table and the parties need more commercial mechanics than Tier 2's minimal overlay provides. The data-protection substance (Sections I, II and III + Annexes I–IV) is unmodified and carries the full Art. 28(7) compliance presumption — the same presumption as Tier 2. Section 4 adds commercial mechanics (term, commercial termination rights, transition, liability allocation, indemnification, insurance) that the Clauses do not address, without touching Clause 10 or any other Clause.

The architecture is defensible to a supervisory authority: Sections I, II and III are unmodified (compliance presumption preserved in full); Section 4 of this DPA is permitted under Clause 2(b) of the Clauses because it does not contradict them (which the SA can verify by reading Section 4 and confirming no Clause 10 or Section II obligation is narrowed, duplicated-with-a-conflict, or made subject to the commercial termination mechanism as an exclusive alternative).

The Hierarchy clause (Clause 4 of the Clauses) still governs: where Section 4 of this DPA were to conflict with the Clauses, the Clauses prevail. Practitioners should walk Section 4 against Clause 10 and Section II clause-by-clause as a final check before signing — a stray indemnity exclusion, liability cap, or termination clause that effectively narrows or overrides a Clause 10 or Section II obligation would create a tension the SA might pick up, and would put the presumption for that provision at risk.
